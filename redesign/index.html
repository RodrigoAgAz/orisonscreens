How it works

One fare, five postcodes, five different ads.

The screen knows where the cab is. As the journey crosses from Mayfair into Soho, into the City, the creative changes with it, and every play is written to a log with a time and a position.

  • TargetingCreative is chosen by where the cab is, not by the slot it was booked into.
  • FrequencyOne passenger, one fare, several messages, each one relevant to the street outside.
  • ProofEvery play written with a time, a zone and a duration, and returned to the advertiser.

    For advertisers and agencies

    The only screen a passenger cannot walk past.

    Roadside digital gets a few seconds of a glance. The back of a cab gets the passenger's full journey, seated, facing the screen, with nowhere else to look.

    Formats

    Full-screen video at 15 and 30 seconds, static, and journey takeovers. Sound is driver-controlled. [Confirm loop length and share of voice]

    Targeting

    By borough, postcode, or a geofence you draw. By daypart and day of week. The creative changes as the cab crosses from the City into Shoreditch.

    Buying

    Programmatic through your existing DSP via [SSP partner – confirm], or a direct insertion order for guaranteed share of voice.

    Proof

    A play log for every impression with time and GPS position. Screens report play only while a passenger is in the cab. [Confirm occupancy detection method]

    The screen

    Fitted into the seat, not stuck onto it.

    Rideshare screens are tablets clipped to a headrest. Ours is built into the tip-up seat back and wired to the vehicle, so there is nothing for a passenger to knock, nothing for a driver to store at the end of a shift, and nothing that looks like an afterthought.
